# Beauty Salon Sales Dialogue 03

> Fictional adaptation for training, not a real salon recording. People, services, prices, and transaction arrangements are fictional and are provided only for agent development practice. The dialogue has been edited and does not constitute medical or skincare advice.

Customer: Let me say upfront that I've had facial care before. When I first bought a package I came often, but then I kept traveling for work and stopped bothering. Now I have lots of visits left, and just thinking about them annoys me.

Consultant: Then let's not rush into how many visits to buy today. What made it hardest to keep coming before: the distance, appointment times, or results that didn't meet your expectations?

Customer: A bit of everything. I'm busy during the week, and weekends are fully booked. It's not that I don't want to spend money; I don't want to spend it and then have to chase opportunities to use it.

Consultant: If I give you another packed schedule, you'll run into the same problem. How much time can you confidently set aside for yourself in the near future?

Customer: I can commit to two visits soon. After that I might be away for a while, and I don't know when I'll be back.

Consultant: Then anything beyond those two visits can't be treated as certain. What are you hoping to get from care this time?

Customer: To feel more comfortable and look less tired. I don't want anything complicated, and I don't want to carry lots of bottles when I travel.

Consultant: Understood: simple routine care that fits your schedule. Do you have any discomfort today, or have you had any other procedures recently?

Customer: No, I'm just busy. I still have plenty of products at home and don't want new ones.

Consultant: Then I won't recommend home-care products today. Specific usage questions would require the instructions for what you already own; I can't suggest new combinations just because you have "some things at home."

Customer: How are you different from other salons? Everyone says they're professional when I ask.

Consultant: You can compare things you can verify: what's included, how long it actually takes, whether appointments can be confirmed, how fees are deducted, and whether you want to continue afterward. Professionalism can't rest on my description alone, and I shouldn't call other services useless when they aren't here to explain.

Customer: Last time they said I could come whenever I wanted, but later they were always full.

Consultant: If we're booking today, we'll check actual openings first rather than just say "anytime." If you want to continue, we'll book the one visit you're sure you can attend rather than fill your future schedule.

Customer: If I leave after two visits, does that mean those visits were wasted?

Consultant: Each visit provides one service, but that doesn't let us promise how long results will last. When you return, we'll discuss your situation again. We won't simply blame a gap on you being uncooperative or require you to buy a whole new package because of it.

Customer: Okay, work out the costs clearly. I saw 240 for a single visit and 880 for four. Is buying four better value?

Consultant: If you use all four, the average is CNY 220 per visit, CNY 20 below the single-visit price. But you can currently commit to only two visits, which cost CNY 480 individually. The package requires CNY 880 upfront, an extra CNY 400 prepaid. You also need to consider its four-month validity and dealing with any unused visits later.

Customer: Can't the unused package visits be refunded?

Consultant: Yes. Under the order terms, each visit deducts CNY 220, and unused visits are refunded at the same rate. If you use two visits and refund the other two, your actual spending is CNY 440, CNY 40 less than two single visits. But you must pay CNY 880 first and apply for a refund. That difference matters too.

Customer: So if I don't want the hassle, I'm paying 40 for flexibility.

Consultant: That's a fair way to describe the cost difference between these options. Unused visits and scheduling were your biggest problems before, so I'd lean toward single visits rather than another package just for the lower average price. The final choice is yours, of course.

Customer: Do you have a two-visit package?

Consultant: There isn't one on the current price list, so I can't invent a discount on the spot. You can pay CNY 240 separately for each of two visits, or decide on just one today.

Customer: Can I use it at another location? It would help if there were one where I travel.

Consultant: The salon in this material doesn't offer cross-location use. I can't promise nationwide use without verifying locations. Today we only promise services this salon can provide.

Customer: All right. I'll book the next visit first. What if something comes up before the second?

Consultant: We can note a preferred time for the second without collecting payment, then book once you confirm. Today's visit also follows the order's rescheduling rules. I'll show you those rules rather than just explain them verbally.

Customer: I don't have much time today either; I have errands afterward.

Consultant: Let's check how long you can stay. If there isn't enough time for the agreed service, we'll choose another time rather than shorten it and charge for the full service.

Customer: I can stay for an hour.

Consultant: This basic-care visit takes about 40 minutes. Including confirmation and preparation, we can plan within that time. I'll let you know promptly if anything changes during the service.

Customer: Fine. I'll decide about another visit after this one.

Consultant: Understood. Today is one visit at CNY 240, with no prepayment for the second and no four-visit package. I'll record that simplicity, comfort, and easy scheduling matter most to you.

Customer: Just don't add me to a promotional group.

Consultant: We won't. If we need to contact you, we'll confirm appointments only in the way you agree to, not turn every contact into a package sales pitch.

Customer: That's fine.
