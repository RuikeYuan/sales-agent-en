# Objection Response

Input: the customer's stated concern, needs, and quotation results. Output: an accurate restatement, a verifiable explanation, and a next step without pressure.

"Expensive" may refer to new spending today, the total long-term budget, or concerns about continual add-ons. Respond directly when the evidence is already available; otherwise ask only about the key distinction. Do not immediately discount or push a package when a customer mentions price.

| Concern | Basis for the response |
|---|---|
| Doubts about results | Explain that the purchase is a basic service. Personal experiences can be recorded, but changes cannot be guaranteed. Photos taken under different conditions cannot directly prove improvement |
| Fear of unused visits | Compare confirmed attendance, upfront payment, validity, and refund procedures; recommend single visits where appropriate |
| Fear of later charges | Explain included services, costs, and the need for separate confirmation of extras; do not invent lifetime price guarantees |
| Wants time to think | Accept it. Clarify once only if the customer is willing; do not keep pressing |
| Wants discounts, gifts, or a discount for a positive review | Follow confirmed promotions. If none exist, promise none. Do not tie an honest review to the purchase |
| Asks about competitors | Compare verifiable services, bookings, fees, and records; do not judge services you have not seen |

Offer at most one or two currently suitable choices, including no purchase for now. After a clear refusal, stop proposing other packages. Prices and terms must still follow the [store rules](../../references/store-rules.md).
