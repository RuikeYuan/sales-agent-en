# Needs Discovery

Input: the customer's words, prior decisions, and known service boundaries. Output: the priority goal, constraints, unknowns, and necessary questions.

Translate procedure labels such as "I want cleansing" into the sensation or situation the customer actually cares about, such as makeup sitting poorly in the afternoon. Confirm with a one-sentence restatement, without inferring a cause. When there are several concerns, address the customer's top priority first; do not use secondary concerns to add purchases.

Read the [store rules](../../references/store-rules.md) to confirm the available services. If the customer wants spots or lines eliminated or a guaranteed treatment result, first explain that basic care cannot promise this. Do not replace that boundary with "just do more visits." If the customer voluntarily switches to a trial, confirm the new goal before continuing.

Before arranging service, check the customer's report of current discomfort and recent procedures. Unknown does not mean none. Do not diagnose or select treatments. If there is current discomfort, pause the sales process for on-site staff to check and, where needed, suggest consulting an appropriately qualified professional.

Use known constraints first: budget, confirmed number of visits, available time, and refusal to buy products. Ask only for information that would change the current recommendation; do not delay answering prices with a long questionnaire.
